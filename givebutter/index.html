Who I am

I started in tech at GiveGab, working directly with nonprofits every day — chat support, then CS, project management, and then implementation. I sat on committees for local nonprofits, helped organize large-scale events, and supported marketing and communications work. I understood what these organizations were actually trying to do, and what it cost them when the tools didn't work.

From there, I made a deliberate move into a different sector. Zappi is data-heavy, enterprise-facing, a genuinely different kind of customer, and a different kind of pressure. I wanted to see what my thinking looked like when I couldn't rely on sector familiarity. What I came back with was sharper — better process instincts, cleaner systems thinking, and a clearer sense of where implementation functions actually break down and why.

At Zappi, that meant inheriting a delivery motion built around what worked for us internally, not what got the customer to value. So we changed it. Stopped asking customers to carry setup decisions they didn't have context for yet. Started leading with recommendations instead of questions. We continue to apply AI at the specific friction points where it adds genuine customer value, and only after the process is clear enough to support it.

50% Reduction in time to first value — 30 days to 15, driven by process clarity and targeted AI.
~38% Implementation time reduction across all implementations, 73 days to 45, with high satisfaction scores throughout.
Same headcount Across a growing implementation volume — the gains came from a complete redesign focused on customer value, not added capacity.

Why Givebutter

Givebutter has done something most platforms can't: deployed the latest in tech successfully to nearly 90K nonprofits in a sector not always first to adopt it. Most platforms at this growth rate start to slip on the customer side. The product gets bloated, onboarding gets sidelined, and the under-resourced nonprofits on the other end start to feel like a number. Givebutter hasn't done that. The ratings, the retention, the community — it's evident.

That's what I want to help protect. Not manage it from the outside. Be part of building the infrastructure that keeps it true as the scale keeps climbing.

What I'd bring

Someone who goes beyond the checklists. Who asks why the playbook exists before deciding whether to keep it or what to improve. Who's done this work in the nonprofit space and beyond, and knows what transfers and what doesn't. Who can sit in the details and still paint the larger picture.

Not someone who runs the system. Someone who pushes the system to customer outcomes.
